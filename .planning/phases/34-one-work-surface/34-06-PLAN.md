<objective>
Move the two organizer-only surfaces — `events/[id]/assignments` and `events/[id]/review` — to
the collapsed address, and finish turning the assignment allow-list from a regex tested
against a prefix into a per-route binding.

Purpose: D-34-03. These two have no `/admin` twin, so this is a move rather than a merge — but
`review` carries `ORGANIZER_ASSIGNMENT_ROUTES` with it, and **that is a Critical edit, not a
move**: the allow-list is the whole safety of that arm. Plan 34-01 wrote the binding
(`party.manage`, `assignmentOpenable: true`) and plan 34-03 deleted the regex. This plan puts
the page at the address the binding names, and proves the per-night gate on the page — which
re-asks `party.manage` against the night resolved from `?party=` — was not disturbed.

Output: two surfaces at `/admin/events/[id]/assignments` and `/admin/events/[id]/review`, with
the `(organizer)` originals deleted.
</objective>

<phase_rule>
**R-WORK-ROUTES — only Next.js route files enter `(work)`.** Declared in plan 34-07, Task 1,
and cited here rather than re-derived. `page.tsx` and `loading.tsx` move into
`src/app/(admin)/admin/(work)/…`; `actions.ts` and co-located client components stay at their
`src/app/(admin)/admin/…` path, outside the group. A moved page reaches its former siblings
with an absolute `@/app/(admin)/admin/…` specifier — an edit inside the page, owned by this
plan. The rule exists because a route group governs routing and nothing else, so moving a
non-route module buys nothing and costs an edit in every file that imports it, several of
which are owned by sibling plans in the same wave.
</phase_rule>
