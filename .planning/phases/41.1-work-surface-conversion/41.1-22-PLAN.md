<impact_analysis>
## Written before the diff — this surface sells tickets

**Classification: Structured, at the boundary of Critical.** These files define
what a ticket costs and what a discount does. They do not move money themselves —
the checkout and the webhook are elsewhere and are not opened — but **a price
field is the input to every amount the system will ever charge**, and a validation
attribute lost in a substitution is a wrong price waiting for a buyer.

**Domain:** `ticketing-payments` primary; `access-gating` and
`nextjs-architecture` secondary.

**What is opened:** the tickets route file and the four ticket components.
**What is not:** `RefundActions.tsx` (converted in plan 41.1-17 with its own
impact analysis), the ticket server actions, the SumUp library, the webhook route
and every cron.

**Cross-domain impact:**
- *Ticketing.* A tier's price, its quantity, its sale window and a discount's
  value and limits are all fields on this surface. Their types, minimums, maximums
  and required flags are **validation, not styling**, and every one is carried
  through unchanged.
- *Access and gating.* The surface is drawn for roles whose write may be refused;
  the refusal paths stay reachable and keep their own sentences.
- *Production calendar.* A tier belongs to a night. Nothing here renumbers or
  re-sorts a series.

## The three stop conditions, verbatim

1. **`venue_reveal_sent` is monotone.** Nothing in this closure reaches the
   reveal; asserted by grep.
2. **A payment reaching `completed` corrects forward.** **No conversion changes a
   status transition, a refund amount, an idempotency key or a webhook path.** A
   plan that finds it cannot convert without doing so **halts and returns to the
   owner.**
3. **A series progressivo is already on a poster.** No conversion renumbers,
   re-sorts or re-derives one — and tier ordering is what a buyer sees, not a
   display preference.
</impact_analysis>
