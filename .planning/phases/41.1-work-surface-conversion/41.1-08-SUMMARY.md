**Why this is not the three-columns case, which is the point of reading per
class.** Pitfall P6 is the three-column count at the small prefix becoming two
columns at the middle tier and three at the large one: a **column count**
skipped its middle step. This is a **track template** —
one flexible track and two `auto` ones, holding an account select, a short job
select and the Assign button. Two of its three tracks size to their content, so
the row does not need three equal columns' worth of width; it needs enough for

navigation column, which that composition fits. Giving it a middle step would
have invented a two-track intermediate that the row has no third state for.

Renaming the prefix without reading the class is what produces three columns in
544px on a tablet; reading it is what produced the same destination the spec's
map already names for this exact class.

## Deviations from plan

### 1. `[Rule 1 — wrong role applied, corrected]` The add-guest heading

**Found during:** task 2's reading of §7, after task 1 was committed.

**Issue:** the form's heading was converted onto the **section label** (mono,
caps, wide tracking). Both readings were defensible and one of them is specific:
§7.1 names the six component headings rendering one step above the heading size
and says what they become when their surface converts — *the heading role*, at
16px/600 in the interface face. This was one of those six.

**Fix:** the heading role, written as a class string (D-41-11: the component is a
convenience and a surface that writes the string is equally converted). It also
keeps a caps transform off a heading one element away from data the brand renders
literally.

**Commit:** `1431677`. **Corrected rather than left**, because a converted
surface carrying the wrong type role is the thing the next reader copies.

### 2. `[Rule 2 — missing critical functionality]` Two controls with no accessible name

**Found during:** task 2.

**Issue:** neither assignment select had **any** accessible name — no label, no
`aria-label`, no `aria-labelledby`. A screen reader announced the control that
hands somebody a job at a door as "combo box" and nothing else. §8.6 makes the
name mandatory by one of two routes and says *"neither is not an option"*.

**Fix:** `aria-label` on both, not a visible label — a visible one would have put
a caption above two of the three tracks and left the Assign button aligned
against nothing, which would have changed the row's shape rather than its
styling.

**Files:** `AssignmentsClient.tsx`. **Commit:** `bfd63a7`.

### 3. `[reported, not fixed]` The plan expected checkboxes on the assignments surface, and there are none

The plan's action says *"every checkbox becomes the `Checkbox` primitive"*, and
T-41.1-27 in its threat register mitigates *"a mis-hit checkbox granting a
capability"* by that substitution.

**Measured:** `grep -n 'checkbox|type="checkbox"|Checkbox'` across
`AssignmentsClient.tsx`, the route file and `actions.ts` → **no match.** The
surface grants a capability through **two selects and a button**, not a
checkbox.

**Nothing was invented to satisfy the sentence.** This is recorded rather than
ticked because a threat-register row whose mitigation has no subject reads, to
the next person, as a mitigation that was performed. The three controls that do
exist all reach the 44px floor through the primitives, and task 3's row 2 is
what confirms it at render — but it should be read as covering *selects and
buttons*, which is what is there.

### 4. `[interpretation]` Every status mark took the neutral badge rung

The seven guest-list statuses each carried a raw palette pair — a blue, a
yellow, a green, a teal, a red and two whites — so a state was told by hue first
and by the word second.

§8.5's badge has two tones and **neither grades an outcome**: `emphasis` means
*look here first* and is a `--sem-done` fill, which on a failed row would say the
opposite of what happened, and `Chip.tsx` is explicit that there is deliberately
no tone per outcome. So **every mark is neutral and the word is the channel** —
which is also §12's rule that colour is never the only one.

**What was checked before accepting the loss:** the one state carrying more than
a word still does. A failed entry prints its own reason under the row in the crit
ink, and that sentence is what an operator acts on. **A person should look at
this on task 3** — it is the one change on these two surfaces that a reviewer
might reasonably want back, and it is written here so it is argued with rather
than discovered.

### 5. `[interpretation]` The status badge's hover tooltip was dropped

`StatusBadge` carried `title={status === "failed" && errorMessage ? … }`. The
badge primitive takes no `title`, and the attribute was a **strict duplicate**:
its condition is the same condition, in the same component, under which the full
error message already renders as visible text under the row. Nothing is
reachable now that was not reachable before, and a `title` tooltip was never
reachable by touch or keyboard in the first place.

### 6. `[interpretation]` The feedback banner keeps `role="alert"` on both tones

The house pattern (`Dialog.tsx:316-322`) is `role="alert"` for the critical tone
and `role="status"` otherwise. **Left unchanged deliberately:** the incumbent is
assertive for both, and making an accepted **capability grant** announce more
quietly than it does today is a behaviour decision on an access surface, not a
styling one. Recorded so the next reader sees a decision rather than an
oversight.
