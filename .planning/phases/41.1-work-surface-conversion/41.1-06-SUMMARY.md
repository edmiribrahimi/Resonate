> convention, which is the funniest possible place and the one nobody greps.

> broken fragment (`grep -coE "\.font-me\{|\.font-semib\{|\.font-b\{|\.dium|\.ium\{"`
> over the built stylesheet → **0**, exit 1). The two retired weights that *do*

## 7. Deviations from plan

### 1. `[Rule 3 — the plan's criterion could not be met as written]` The empty state keeps its inline padding

Written out in §6 above. §8.11's contract and the named analog both carry it;
the criterion's target is the page gutter, which is zero.

### 2. `[Rule 2 — missing critical assertion]` The palette grep was widened before it was trusted

The plan's palette pattern names **seven** colour families. `ComposeForm.tsx`
carried a neutral-grey ground on its preview header — a raw palette utility from
a family the pattern does not name, which would have passed the criterion and
failed the intent. The grep was re-run against **all 22** Tailwind palette
families, and that is the run reported in §6. The utility is converted to the
raised ground token.

A criterion that names its examples rather than its property is a criterion that
passes the case nobody thought of, and this is the second time in this phase
family a pattern has been narrower than the thing it was written to catch.

### 3. `[Rule 2 — accessibility, and it is why the substitution earns its diff]` Two form fields had no accessible name

Both labels in `ComposeForm.tsx` were bare `<label>` elements with no `htmlFor`
and no wrapping, so neither field had a programmatic name. This was not in the
plan as a defect — it describes the field conversion as a boundary-contrast
change — but it is the larger of the two effects and it is the §8.6 primitive's
declared job. Fixed by the substitution itself. Also `role="alert"` added to the
validation complaint, which §11 requires of every error region and which it
lacked.

### 4. `[interpretation]` `NewsletterClient.tsx` was measured clean and given a docblock rather than a rewrite

It is in `files_modified`, and the frontmatter is an authorization, not an
obligation to dirty a file. It needed no class change. The edit records the
measurement in the file, so that *clean* stays distinguishable from *unopened*
for whoever next audits the closure.

written here so it is not discovered a third time.

### 8.1 `provider_unavailable` is one tag for four causes — the recorded anti-pattern, one level down

`FailureNotice.tsx` tells three causes apart and its copy is good; **that is not
the whole of `CONCERNS.md`'s finding.** The tag `provider_unavailable` covers
every way the mail provider can fail — a missing key, a rejected key, a rate
limit, and an address already subscribed — and all four arrive with one title
and one instruction to check two environment variables. That is the same
collapsed-cause shape at a narrower scale.

It lives in `actions.ts`, not in the component. **The copy was not rewritten**
(§11: *"none introduced"*, and the plan says so explicitly), and the split is a
behavioural change that does not belong inside a conversion commit. Owner
decision, or its own plan.

This matters more than it looks: `meta-gates.md` records that **this project has
no error tracking**, so a cause that is not distinguishable on screen is not
distinguishable anywhere.

### 8.2 The newsletter page title reads `Admin`

The surface is the newsletter and its `<h1>` says `Admin`. Every other work page
names itself. It is copy, §11 introduces none, and a visual plan that quietly
renames a surface has made a product decision in a styling commit. The string is
unchanged; the correction is a one-word decision for whoever owns the copy.

### 8.3 The broadcast status mark lost its hue, deliberately

The incumbent drew `sent` in green and `draft` in amber from the raw palette.
The badge primitive has exactly **two** tones and states in its own docblock that
it *"does not grade an outcome, and there is deliberately no tone per outcome"* —
`emphasis` means *look here first*, not *this one went well*. Both marks are now
neutral and the **word inside them is the channel**, which is §10's rule that
colour is never the only one, reached from the other side.

**A reader loses a hue and loses no information.** If the owner wants the two
graded by colour, that is a contract decision — a third badge tone — and not a
call site's to make.

### 8.4 The delete control was drawing on a reserved token

It was a ~16px text control in the accent. §5.1 reserves the accent for four
things — the primary button fill, the active navigation entry, a link inside
prose, and the lineup pills — and a destructive row control is none of them. It
is now a ghost button at the small rung: 44px, the shared focus ring, a recessed
ink. **No confirmation was added**: there is none today, adding one is
behavioural, and §11's destructive-confirmation rule governs the three
confirmations that exist rather than manufacturing a fourth. Whether deleting a
draft broadcast deserves one is a real question and it is not this plan's.

### 8.5 The email body's six literal hex values are untouched

`ComposeForm.tsx`'s `wrappedHtml` carries six hex values in inline styles. They
are the **email** palette, read by mail clients that support no custom property,
and `DI-40-01` defers the email palette explicitly as an owner decision. A
conversion that tokenised them would have changed what lands in somebody's inbox
from a plan whose whole claim is that it changes nothing that runs.

### 8.6 `DEF-41-01` is still open and still governs every comment written here

Every comment added to the seven files describes utilities in prose and spells
none, per `PageShell.tsx:64-67` and `(work)/layout.tsx:138-141`. The build was
grepped for a malformed-rule warning after both tasks: none.
