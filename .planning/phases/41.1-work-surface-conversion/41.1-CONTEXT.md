## Implementation Decisions

### The navigation clearance — and why DEF-41-08 is not the Critical intervention it looked like

Round 5 handed this phase a structural question it declared too dangerous to
answer inside a gate-authoring round: *the clearance reaches the four focus
routes from outside the shell, and the resolution edits files that are
`access-gating` and `ticketing-payments` primary.* **Measured on the live tree,
that premise does not hold.**

- **D-41.1-01: `--nav-inset-inline-start` stops being ambient.** `:root` declares
  it `0px` unconditionally; the sites that mount the **responsive** navigation
  form declare `14rem` at the `md` tier on the wrapper around their content.

  **The measurement that decides it.** There are **13 navigation mount sites**.
  Only **two** mount `AppNav` in its responsive form — the one that becomes a
  224px leading column at `md` and up:

  | Mount site | Form | Column at `md`+ |
  |---|---|---|
  | `src/app/(admin)/admin/(work)/layout.tsx` | responsive | yes |
  | `src/app/(public)/gallery/page.tsx` | responsive | yes |
  | 11 others, all via `MobileNav` | phone, locked | **no, at any width** |

  `src/app/globals.css:300-305` declares `--nav-inset-inline-start: 14rem` at
  `@media (min-width: 48rem)` on `:root`. **That statement is already false for
  eleven of the thirteen mount sites**, and false for every route that mounts no
  navigation at all. Nothing breaks visibly today only because the eleven
  hard-code `pb-24` and never read the variable. DEF-41-08's reproduction — a
  route wrapper above the auth group that reads the variable and receives 224px —
  is not a hole waiting to open; it is the first place an already-false ambient
  default produced a visible effect.

  With the default at zero, a wrapper anywhere that reads the variable resolves to
  nothing. **The clearance becomes structurally unable to reach a route that
  mounts no navigation** — which is DEF-41-08's resolution (2), the one round 5
  called the owner's.

- **D-41.1-02: only the inline axis moves. `--nav-inset-block-end` stays
  ambient.** The bar *is* mounted at phone width on twelve of the thirteen sites,
  so the ambient value is approximately true where it is read; `PageShell`'s focus
  branch does not read it; and moving it would change `Dialog`'s sheet padding and
  `ToastContainer`'s offset on unconverted 41.2 surfaces **to fix no measured
  defect**. Narrowing the change to the axis that is wrong is the opposite of the
  five-round pattern chase — and the asymmetry is stated: forgetting the inline
  declaration puts content under a column (loud, first look); leaving the block
  axis alone changes nothing at all.

- **D-41.1-03: `:has()` was considered and rejected, and the reason is not
  browser support.** `:root:has([data-app-nav])` would have expressed the exact
  invariant with zero mount-site edits. It is **Baseline widely available since
  December 2023**, and Tailwind v4's own floor — Chrome 111, Safari 16.4, Firefox
  128 — already sits above it, so it would have added no new boundary. It is
  rejected because **MDN explicitly warns against anchoring `:has()` to `:root` or
  `body`**: any DOM change in the subtree forces re-evaluation, and this product
  re-renders a live attendee list on a phone at a door. Written down so the next
  reader does not re-derive it as an oversight.

- **D-41.1-04: the impact analysis, stated before the diff.** Under D-41.1-01 the
  files that change are `src/app/globals.css`,
  `src/app/(admin)/admin/(work)/layout.tsx` and `src/app/(public)/gallery/page.tsx`
  — **two of them already converted, all three in this phase's own territory.**
  **Zero files under `src/app/(auth)/**`, zero under
  `src/app/(public)/payment/callback/**`, zero behind the Phase 42 fence, zero
  unconverted 41.2 pages.** This work is therefore **Structured, not Critical**.

  **And that boundary is a stop condition, not a prediction.** If planning or
  execution discovers the fix cannot land without opening a file under the auth
  group or the payment callback, that is a **halt and a fresh impact analysis put
  to the owner** — not a judgement made at execution time. The delegation covers
  choosing the approach; it does not pre-approve a Critical path the approach was
  chosen to avoid.

- **D-41.1-05: check E stops freezing a shape and starts asserting a pairing.**
  Five consecutive rounds taught the matcher one more shape and watched the defect
  move. With the ambient value gone, *reading* the variable is harmless and the
  property that matters becomes **who declares the column clearance** — a closed
  set with one token. The assertion becomes: *the files declaring the column
  clearance are exactly the files mounting the responsive navigation form.* Local,
  textual, and reachable by a grep — the first form of this check in five rounds
  that is not chasing a shape. The exact assertion is the planner's.

- **D-41.1-06: the door gains no column clearance, and D-41-21's fence holds
  verbatim.** `MobileNav` stays the bar-locked wrapper; `DoorSurface.tsx` and
  `src/app/(admin)/door/**` are not opened. Any mechanism that would give the door
  a leading column at tablet width is rejected on that ground alone — a tablet at
  an entrance losing 224px is a change to the door's surface, and the door is
  Phase 42's.

### The five tables

- **D-41.1-12: the five adopt `src/components/ui/DataTable.tsx`; no table invents
  a second card layout.** The contract already exists and is written —
  `DataCardSlot = "title" | "subtitle" | "mark" | "meta" | "none"`, one renderer
  serving both branches *"so there is nowhere for the two to disagree"*. What
  remains is per-table slot assignment, which is a judgement about which fact is a
  row's identity, not an open design question. The five: `MemberSpendTable` (8
  columns), `DrinkSalesBreakdown` (6), `ReferralChainTable` (4), `SalesDashboard`
  (7), `TransactionList` (6, and 835 lines).

- **D-41.1-13: on a surface that carries money, a figure that decides money is a
  `mark`, never `meta`.** `TransactionList` is the finance transaction list and
  `SalesDashboard` is the event's sales; a card that buries an amount among the
  meta line is a card that changes what an operator reads first. `none` is
  permitted only where the card carries the fact another way — the primitive's own
  docblock already says so, and this decision only refuses the money exception.

- **D-41.1-14: `ReviewListClient` stays exempt, unchanged (D-41-16).** It is the
  copy-out diagnostic grid, and its own copy says it exists to be pasted into a
  diagnostic tool. Cards would destroy the thing it is for.

- **D-41.1-15: a wave's plans partition files, and a shared component two plans
  would both touch is either converted in an earlier wave as spine, or assigned to
  exactly one plan.** `DEF-41-03` records this failure twice in one phase: two
  plans in one wave each satisfied their own contract and left a joint obligation
  that belonged to neither afterwards, and nothing in the workflow looks for one.
  21 pages will parallelise harder than 41 did.

- **D-41.1-16: after each wave merges, one task re-runs the gates and reconciles

  count that fell is not evidence that work happened.

- **D-41.1-17: the plan that removes the `(work)` layout shim is named at planning
  time, not discovered.** Its own docblock already declares the exit — *"removed by

  layout **declares** the clearance and `PageShell` **consumes** it; the shim's two

  converts the layout must keep padding as well, because an unconverted page does
  not render `PageShell` and would otherwise sit under the column. The shim
  therefore shrinks in two steps, and the second one is the last conversion plan.

### Added 2026-08-13, after the research

These four were produced by `41.1-RESEARCH.md`, which measured the tree against
the decisions above and found three of them built on figures that were wrong.
**They are recorded as corrections, not as additions** — a context decision built
on a wrong measurement is worth more corrected than obeyed.

- **D-41.1-19 (owner decision, 2026-08-13): the three knots the work surface
  reaches — the event form, the refund controls and the venue reveal — come into
  this phase, each as its own plan in the last wave, each carrying a written
  impact analysis before its diff.**

  *(One taxonomy, used everywhere. The roadmap originally counted the knots as
  **the bar, the event form, the refunds**, with `RevealVenueDialog` named
  separately. After this decision the split is: **the bar stays in 41.2**, and the
  three that the work surface reaches — form, refunds, reveal — are this phase's.
  Every plan and both roadmap sections now use those words, because two counts of
  the same thing in one document is how a reader concludes one of them is wrong.)*

  **The contradiction this resolves.** This document said, three lines apart,
  *"in scope: the 21 pages and everything their import closures reach"* and *"out
  of scope: the event form, the refunds, `RevealVenueDialog`"*. Both cannot hold:
  criterion 1 is enforced by a gate whose definition of **whole** *is* the import
  closure, and six of the 21 pages reach `EventForm.tsx` (1 669 lines, 148 class
  attributes), `RefundDialog.tsx`, `RefundActions.tsx`, `VenueRevealPanel.tsx` and
  `RevealVenueDialog.tsx`. A conversion plan for `/events/new` that leaves
  `EventForm.tsx` alone **cannot declare `/events/new` converted** — check A walks
  into it and fails on its palette hits.

  **A file-level split was priced and is not available**, and that is measured,
  not argued: check A walks *through* `VenueRevealPanel.tsx` into
  `RevealVenueDialog.tsx`. Independently re-verified before the decision was put
  to the owner (`grep -rln '/RevealVenueDialog"' src` → one importer, and it is
  `VenueRevealPanel.tsx`, which `/events/[id]/edit` imports).

  **The alternative and why it lost.** Narrowing criterion 1 would leave six of
  21 pages unconverted here — and the `(work)` layout shim protects exactly those
  six, so the shim could not be removed either, failing criterion 1's second half
  and overruling D-41.1-17. One resolution keeps both halves of the criterion; the
  other keeps neither.

  **What is kept from the roadmap's original reasoning.** *"Prove it on the
  hardest correct file"* is not *"convert the most dangerous surface first."* The
  knots are last **within this phase**, after fifteen ordinary pages have proven
  the pattern — not first, and not scattered through the parallel waves.

  **The authorized file list, and it is a list rather than a principle.** Five
  files were named when the decision was taken: `EventForm.tsx`,
  `RefundDialog.tsx`, `RefundActions.tsx`, `VenueRevealPanel.tsx`,
  `RevealVenueDialog.tsx`.

  **A sixth was added on 2026-08-13 by a separate owner decision:**
  `src/app/(public)/events/[slug]/menu/EventQRCode.tsx`. It reached the plans
  first as an engineering inference — the file was measured (69 lines; props are
  `url` and `eventTitle` only; no amount, no address, no payment path) and treated
  as settled. **The plan checker refused that**, correctly: a measurement showing a
  crossing is *safe* is not the owner having *authorized* it, and this file sits
  under `(public)` and is shared with the bar's menu page, which is 41.2's.

  The decision was then put and taken on its own terms. Its reason is the same
  topology as the five: `/events/[id]/drinks` reaches it, so without it that page
  cannot be declared converted and criterion 1 breaks on one more page. The bar's
  menu page inherits an already-converted component in 41.2, which is the harmless
  direction — the reverse would not be.

  **The rule this establishes for the rest of the phase:** any further file
  outside this list that a closure turns out to reach is **an owner decision, not
  a plan-level inference**, however small and however well measured. The list
  grows by decision or not at all.

  **The three stop conditions they bring, written so they are refused rather than
  discovered:**

  1. **`venue_reveal_sent` is monotone.** Work on `VenueRevealPanel.tsx` or
     `RevealVenueDialog.tsx` may only make the reveal **harder** to trigger, never
     easier — and changing a confirmation's `autofocus` target, its button variant
     or its copy *is* touching that guard. `41-UI-SPEC.md` §11 already fixes the
     rule: the cancel is the default and the `autofocus` target; no
     Enter-to-confirm, no autofocus on the destructive control.
  2. **A payment reaching `completed` corrects forward.** `/finance`,
     `/events/[id]/sales` and `/events/[id]/tickets` render refund controls. **No
     conversion changes a status transition, a refund amount, an idempotency key
     or a webhook path.** A plan that finds it cannot convert without doing so
     halts and returns to the owner.
  3. **A series progressivo is already on a poster.** No conversion renumbers,
     re-sorts or re-derives one. Nothing in the 21 pages does today; stated so it
     stays true.

### Added 2026-08-13, after wave 3 — three decisions taken under owner delegation

Wave 3 surfaced three questions. They were put to the owner and returned as
*"expert persona decides"*. **Two of the three answers reversed once the code was
read rather than reasoned about**, which is why the measurement is recorded beside
each.

- **D-41.1-25: the status badge does NOT gain outcome tones. The contract holds
  and the lost colour is an accepted loss.**

  Two conversions independently dropped status colour — the newsletter's broadcast
  marks and the guest list's per-status coding — because `Badge` has `neutral` and
  `emphasis` and nothing that grades an outcome. The obvious fix was to add
  outcome tones from the semantic scale, which **already exists and is already
  exposed**: `--sem-crit`, `--sem-warn`, `--sem-info`, `--sem-done` are declared in
  `globals.css` and mapped into `@theme inline`, so the utilities are live today.

  **The obvious fix is refused, because the absence is a decision with its reason
  written in the file.** `Chip.tsx`'s own docblock states it: the `emphasis` fill
  means *look here first*, it **does not grade an outcome**, and *"there is
  deliberately no tone per outcome"* — because the first surface to render it is
  the membership register, and *"assigning a colour to each of the seven acts
  would settle in CSS what that module says is not yet written — the criterion by
  which somebody is let in or refused. A refusal is a communication, not a hue."*
  That is `community-membership.md`'s *nessuna corsia grigia* gate, reached
  through the visual system.

  Overturning a written, reasoned contract decision from the previous phase, in
  the middle of a conversion, on the strength of two executors' visual
  observation, is exactly the move this project's own rule forbids: a superseded
  decision without its reason reads as an oversight. **DS-04's requirement is met
  either way — the word is the channel, and colour was never allowed to be the
  only one.**

  **One candidate is left open rather than folded in.** A *newsletter broadcast
  result* is a machine outcome — sent, failed, queued — and carries no judgement
  about a person, so the docblock's reason does not obviously reach it. That is
  the one place the question may legitimately be re-asked, and the criterion that
  distinguishes it is written here so the next reader does not re-litigate the
  whole rule to get at the one case: **does the status grade a person, or report a
  machine?** Open, and deliberately not decided inside a conversion commit.

- **D-41.1-26: `Chip` becomes generic on its route — and the button ladder's bare
  anchor is NAMED, not fixed here.**

  Two different things were reported as one:

  1. **`Chip.href: Route`** is non-generic, so a dynamic segment collapses at the
     default type parameter and a template-literal route will not typecheck. Plan
     41.1-07 hand-composed six links rather than cast (the file's docblock forbids
     the cast: it disables the only check that makes a dead address a build error)
     or generify spine mid-wave (D-41.1-15). **Making it generic is type-only —
     zero runtime change, so no converted surface needs re-observing.** It is
     therefore safe to do as spine work owned by exactly one plan, and it removes
     the hand-composition for every remaining wave.
  2. **`Button` with `href` renders a bare `<a>`** (`Button.tsx:225-228`), and its
     `href` is typed `string`, not `Route` — so it neither client-navigates nor
     participates in dead-link checking. **This is not a defect phase 41
     introduced:** the docblock records that two of the three sites it first
     replaced were already `<a href>` elements, so it preserved behaviour rather
     than changing it.

     **But it is a trap for every conversion still to come**, because a new call
     site reaching for `Button href` on an internal route silently gets a full
     document load in an App Router application. Changing it is a **runtime**
     change to spine and does not belong in a reconciliation wave.

     **Decided: name it, do not change it.** For the rest of this phase, a
     converted surface that needs an internal navigation control uses `Chip` or
     `Link` — **never `Button href`**. That closes the trap without a spine
     behaviour change mid-conversion, and the change itself is deferred with its
     measurement attached.

- **D-41.1-27: the typographic measure gets its own vocabulary, and check D is
  NOT narrowed.** `DEF-41-04` has claimed a second victim — the format
  catalogue's explanatory paragraph in phase 41, the venue bio in wave 3 — and
  both times the page changed rather than the contract, because check D's matcher
  cannot tell a **container maximum** (the shell's, never a page's) from a
  **typographic measure** (a property of a text block, which no tier owns).

  The tempting fix — narrow the matcher so it stops flagging a measure on a `<p>`
  — is **refused**: narrowing makes the gate find *less*, and a genuine container
  maximum written on an inner element would stop being caught. That is the silent
  direction, and this phase has spent four mechanisms learning to distrust it.

  **Instead the measure gains a declared form** the gate recognises, so a measure
  is *stated* rather than *inferred*, and any undeclared width on a converted page
  still fails. The exemption is written into the gate **before** its first red run,
  with the reason travelling with the entry — the house rule, and the thing that
  keeps a gate trusted instead of switched off.

  **This closes on a schedule, not on goodwill:** without it nothing reminds
  anyone, because the gate is green and a paragraph that got wider does not
  protest.

- **D-41.1-28: the toast row is closed by measurement, not deferred — and
  D-41.1-03 now rests on a number instead of a prediction.**

  Plan 41.1-11 correctly separated one H41-1 row as **non-deferrable**: the owner
  accepted the 32px overlap on 2026-08-13, but on a guest list that had **not yet
  been converted**, and if a second look dissented the evidence would land on
  `ToastContainer.tsx` — which is **spine**, and spine is what every remaining
  wave builds on. By the deferral rule's own criterion (does the outcome change
  what a later plan writes?) that row blocks and the other 48 do not.

  **It was settled without a session, because the toast cannot be moved by the
  page underneath it.** `ToastContainer` is `position: fixed` with `left: 0`,
  `right: 0`, centring a fixed-maximum child, and it is mounted at the root above
  any wrapper that declares the column clearance. Its geometry is therefore a
  function of the **viewport and the stylesheet only**. That is what makes
  `/gallery` — public, converted, and mounting the same responsive navigation — a
  valid stand-in for the guest list, and it is why the executor's worry (*the page
  changed shape under the toast between the two sittings*) dissolves rather than
  needing a second sitting: **the page's shape cannot move it.**

  Measured at render over CDP, reproducing the container's own declared styles and
  measuring against the real navigation element:

  | Width | Column ends | Toast occupies | Overlap |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **768px** | 224px | 192–576 | **32px** |
  | 1024px | 224px | 320–704 | **0** |
  | 1440px | 224px | 528–912 | **0** |

  **768px is the only width at which any overlap exists**, and it is exactly the
  32px that was accepted. Above 1024px the toast clears the column entirely. The
  off-centre reads 112px at every width — half the column, exactly — which is
  arithmetic, not a defect.

  **D-41.1-03 stays closed**, and it now rests on a measurement that is strictly
  better news than what was accepted on a prediction. `:has()` stays refused on
  MDN's performance guidance, and the alternative's true price is one width.

- **D-41.1-29: the semantic set cannot carry two distinguishable outcome fills —
  measured, and it closes wave 5's one blocking row.**

  Plan 41.1-13 flagged one H41-1 row as non-deferrable: *are `--sem-done` and
  `--sem-crit` tellable apart **as fills**?* It is spine evidence, and every
  remaining conversion that grades two outcomes inherits the answer.

  **Computed from the declared token values** (`globals.css`), so it needed no
  session and no eye:

  | Pair | Ratio |
  |---|---|
  | `--sem-done` `#9B7BE0` **vs** `--sem-crit` `#FF6B8E` | **1.23 : 1** |
  | `--sem-done` on `--ground` | 5.99 : 1 |
  | `--sem-crit` on `--ground` | 7.36 : 1 |

  **1.23 : 1 against WCAG 1.4.11's 3 : 1 for distinguishing a UI component.** The
  two work well as ink, and as a fill carrying ground-coloured text — that is what
  the `emphasis` badge already does at 5.99. They do **not** work as two fills a
  reader tells apart at a glance. The row's answer is **no**.

  **This reinforces D-41.1-25 from a direction it was not decided on.** That
  decision refused outcome tones on a *policy* ground — *a refusal is a
  communication, not a hue*. This measurement adds an independent one: **the
  palette cannot do the other thing anyway.** Two of four semantic tokens sit
  1.23 : 1 apart, and `globals.css:169-173` already records that adding a green is
  the owner's decision. So "the word is the channel" is not a preference the
  conversions settled for — it is the only thing the token set currently supports.

  Recorded so no later wave re-opens it on the assumption that a colour pair was
  available and simply not used.

- **D-41.1-30 (OPEN — owner's, and it is the one real gap wave 5 found): this
  design system has no colour vocabulary for a chart series.**

  Two plans reached it independently and **both refused to fill it**, which is the
  behaviour that makes it worth recording rather than a nuisance:

  - It is **not a format** — DS-02 confines format colours to where a format is
    identified.
  - It is **not a state** — the four semantic tokens are excluded by D-41.1-25,
    and by D-41.1-29 they could not carry a four-step series anyway.
  - `--accent`'s reserved-for list in §5.1 names four things, and a chart bar is
    on neither that list nor the never-list — **so the token already filling bars
    on two surfaces is outside its own list.**

  **And the values are invisible to every gate.** Measured at two independent
  sites: a four-step palette (one token reference, three literal hexes) in
  `src/lib/analytics/event-queries.ts`, and per-chart colour props in the wave-5
  components. Both carry **zero class attributes**, and every gate under
  `scripts/` reads class strings — so raw palette colour travelling as **data on a
  row or a render prop** is a blind spot of the whole gate family, the same
  species as the `loading.tsx` gap D-41.1-21 closed.

  **Neither agent invented a palette**, and both said why in the same words:
  choosing four colours inside a component is the system acquiring a rule nobody
  decided, and the next three chart surfaces would copy it.

  **What is owed:** a decision on whether a chart series gets its own scale, and —
  separately — whether the gate family should be able to see a colour that never
  becomes a class. **Neither is this phase's**, and neither should be settled
  inside a conversion commit. Written here so the third chart surface does not
  quietly answer it by precedent.

- **D-41.1-24 CORRECTED, 2026-08-14, by the plan that executed against it.** The
  decision said the venue confirmation's focus placement was *"unspecified in
  practice"* because `RevealVenueDialog`'s `autoFocus` prop is inert. **The inert
  half is true; the conclusion was not.** Sixteen lines above that prop, the
  dialog's open effect already called `.focus()` on the cancel ref **immediately
  after `showModal()`** — the moment that lands. **Cancel was already focused
  before this phase touched it.**

  The pattern map read the attribute and stopped short of the live call, and this
  decision inherited the conclusion without re-reading. Recorded rather than
  quietly amended, because it is the second time in this phase family that a
  belief about a file survived because nobody re-derived it.

  **What actually tightened**, and all three are verifiable: two mechanisms became
  one (an inert prop beside a live call is bait for the next reader); **the focus
  became visible**, since the hand-rolled buttons declared no focus expression at
  all — so *"cancel is focused"* was true **and unobservable**; and the refusal
  left the scroller, so it can no longer land below the fold.

  The stop condition it carried was right for the wrong reason, and it still
  stands: *an argument that something is tighter is not the observation that it
  is.*

- **D-41.1-31 (OPEN — three safety findings the knots surfaced, none of them a
  conversion, all correctly refused inside conversion commits).**

  Wave 7 was the wave that touched money and the monotone guard. Its three
  executors each found the same *shape* of defect on a different surface: **a
  control that causes something irreversible, reached in fewer steps than a
  control that causes nothing.**

  1. **Approving a refund moves money on a single press, with no confirmation —
     while *rejecting* one, which moves nothing, takes two.** The safety asymmetry
     is inverted. `ticketing-payments.md`'s standing rule is that money never
     trusts the thing that announces it; here the interface trusts a single click.
  2. **The venue-secrecy toggle has no confirmation in front of it**, and the flag
     it writes is one of the three monotone guards: an address, once out, does not
     come back. The conversion enlarged its hit area from 24 to 44px — which is a
     **mis-hit correction, not an invitation** — and the executor said so rather
     than letting the enlargement read as an endorsement.
  3. **`Remove` deletes a night with no confirmation**, and `updateEvent` really
     deletes it. **That night may carry a series progressivo already printed on a
     poster** — `production-calendar.md`'s numbering guard, reached from a form.
     The executor deliberately kept that control's visual weight *low* so that
     *bigger* would not also mean *more inviting*.

  **All three are behaviour changes on guarded paths, so none was made.** That is
  the correct call three times over: a conversion commit is the worst place in the
  project to add or remove a confirmation step, because nobody reviews it as one.

  **What is owed:** a decision on each, taken as a safety change and reviewed as
  one. They are recorded together because the pattern is worth more than the three
  instances — **this product's irreversible controls are, on average, easier to
  reach than its reversible ones**, and that is not something any gate in this
  repository can see.
