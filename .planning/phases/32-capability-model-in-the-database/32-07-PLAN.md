<objective>
Replace the five inherited predicates with calls to the one resolver, in place, under
the existing policy names — 45 call sites across 43 policies, and not one policy more.

Purpose: this is where the phase earns CAP-01. Until now the model existed and
answered nothing. After this, a row-level policy and the middleware reach the same
verdict from the same definition.

It is also where the phase can commit its single unrecoverable defect. Thirty-four
policies ignore `status` and four require `approved`. **They map to two different
capabilities.** A cutover that maps them to one silently grants a pending organizer
insert and update on `venues` and `artists`, or silently starts requiring approval on
thirty-four others. Either is a CAP-03 defect, and the write matrix is what catches it.

Output: one applied migration, one derived mapping document.
</objective>

<phase_decisions>
- **D-02** — two organizer shapes, two capabilities. `staff.manage` for the 34,
  `catalogue.manage` for the four. Not negotiable at execution time.
- **D-12** — policy names never change; predicates are replaced **in place** with
  `DROP POLICY IF EXISTS` immediately followed by `CREATE POLICY` under the same name,
  the same `cmd`, the same `permissive` value and the same `TO` clause. B1 compares
  `policyname`; a rename destroys the comparison. The repo's naming convention
  (`20260224_rbac_migration.sql:140-142`) governs only a policy created from nothing,
  and this plan creates none.
- **D-26** — the mapping is **derived from the committed B1 dump**, not typed from
  the research document. Postgres's own rendering is the authority on what is applied;
  a hand-typed list is how the 46th policy gets forgotten.
- **D-27** — `auth.uid()` occurrences are **not** wrapped in this migration, except
  where the surrounding predicate is deleted outright by the capability replacement.
  Wrapping is plan `32-08`. One transformation per migration keeps each diff readable
  against exactly one whitelist entry.
</phase_decisions>
