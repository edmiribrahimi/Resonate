<objective>
Three faces, each doing its own job — and the brand's spelling in the two places a
**machine** reads it and a surface conversion never will.

D-40-09 names the defect precisely: `globals.css:31` binds `body` to the **display**
face, so the whole product renders prose in a geometric display face. That is why
`menu/page.tsx:19-21` had to escape it locally, with the reason written beside it:
*"neutral, highly readable in low-light venues"* — and low-light venues are the
door's world. This plan inverts the default rather than multiplying the escape.

Purpose: DS-05's three roles, DS-06's product half, and DS-10's clause 1 — one
stylesheet holding every token, which becomes a **directory listing** rather than a
claim once the menu page's local font import is gone.
Output: Inter wired at the root with a variable, the local escape deleted, `body`
on the interface role, the manifest and the viewport chrome on `#0A0712`, and the
manifest's rule given a permanent reader.

**The trap this plan exists to avoid.** Today's Inter call has **no `variable`
option** — it returns a `className`, not a custom property. A task written as
"move the import" produces a `--font-sans` that resolves to nothing, silently
(RESEARCH §5 P1). The correct shape already exists three lines above it in the
same file: the Orbitron call. Make Inter symmetrical with it.
</objective>
