<objective>
This is criterion 2 of the phase, and the ROADMAP is explicit that it is bought
at a price paid in criterion 3.

The rule: `role NOT IN ('master','organizer','staff') OR status = 'approved'`,
enforced by a **named `CHECK` constraint** on `public.profiles`. The mechanism is
not a preference. `43-RESEARCH.md` measured, in a `postgres:17.6` container, that
a CHECK is not bypassed by `SECURITY DEFINER`, not by superuser, and not by
`session_replication_role = 'replica'`; a trigger is bypassable by all three and
can be disabled by the table **owner**, not only by a superuser. Un-bypassability
is the whole value of D-04, so the CHECK wins and the trigger alternative is
recorded as considered and refused.

The price was built in plan 43-03 and is collected here by flipping one boolean.
The relaxation is `DROP CONSTRAINT` before the seed and `ADD CONSTRAINT … NOT
VALID` after it — the only route that works with a CHECK, with the `NOT VALID`
mandatory because four rows violate the predicate by the time the restore runs.

**The trap this plan must refuse.** Once the constraint exists,
`door.operate`'s `requires_approved = false` will look redundant. It is not
removed. The constraint protects the database; that flag protects the night from
the day the constraint is relaxed for one special case, and refusing a valid
staff member at the door in front of a queue stays worse than the alternative.

Purpose: ROLE-02 and ROLE-03, together, because neither survives without the other.
Output: one constraint, one flipped declaration, four refused writes observed.
</objective>
