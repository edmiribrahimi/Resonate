  <action>
    Create `src/app/(admin)/admin/calendar/dates.ts`.

    Export `formatCivilDate(date: string): string` producing the form `Tue 12 May 2026` —
    weekday abbreviated to three letters, day, month abbreviated to three letters,
    four-digit year — from the `YYYY-MM-DD` parts against fixed weekday and month tables.
    Reuse `isoWeekday` from `@/lib/production/ics/anchors` rather than re-deriving it: one
    weekday computation in the phase, not two.

    Export `formatCivilTimeRange(start, end)` producing `22:00 → 06:00`, 24-hour, with no
    timezone suffix — the file holds floating local times and the surface must not imply a
    precision the data does not have.

    Export `formatProgressivo(n: number | null): string` producing three digits — `001` —
    never stripped to `1`, never localised, never given a thousands separator. A progressivo
    is on a poster; the app shows what is on the poster.

    **The rule, and it is the module's reason for existing:** no `new Date`, no
    `toISOString`, no `toLocaleDateString`, no `Intl.DateTimeFormat`, no timezone library,
    anywhere in this file or anywhere under the calendar surface. Write the reason beside
    it: the rule a reader is checking is *the listing is a Tuesday*, and a surface that
    prints a numeric date forces the reader to reconstruct the weekday — which is where the
    day-count mental model comes back, the model that once reported a perfectly correct
    night as out of rule.

    Reject an input that is not `YYYY-MM-DD` by returning a stable placeholder AND
    surfacing it as a distinguishable value the caller can branch on — never by silently
    printing something plausible.
  </action>
