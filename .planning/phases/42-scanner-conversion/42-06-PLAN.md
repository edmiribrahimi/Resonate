<objective>
Give the door's three outcomes their measured colours, in **both** places they
are drawn, and replace two sentences in the source that assert the opposite of
what is true.

Purpose: the third scan state and the connectivity pill sit about 10 apart at
normal vision and about 2 under deuteranopia — two screens a person in a hurry
can mistake for one another, at an entrance, in the dark, one-handed. Beside that
defect sit **two** comments, in two files, each declaring that the collision was
avoided by choosing amber over yellow. It was not. A comment did not prevent the
defect; it asserted its absence, twice, and the next reader believed it.

The change is **not one line**, and that is the finding this plan exists to
carry: the scan history redraws the same three states with the same three SVG
paths at `ScannerClient.tsx:3282-3320`. Change only the flash and the verdict
says violet while the record of the same scan, three seconds later on the same
surface, says amber.

The connectivity pill is lifted into a named constant in the same pass — not
tidying, but the only way the gate can **read** it: today the raw yellow appears
ten times across five different features and the pill has no unique textual
anchor, so a gate looking for it would be guessing, and a guessing gate prints a
green over a measurement it did not make.

Output: three fills, one ink, six glyphs still identical, two corrected
paragraphs, one constant — and a legibility gate that exits 0 for the first time.
</objective>
