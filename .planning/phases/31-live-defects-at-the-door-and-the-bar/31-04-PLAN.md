<objective>
Give the night a durable record, give a refund evidence that outlives its ticket,
and let one person be recorded at two parties of one event.

Four schema facts stand in the way of six requirements:

1. **There is nowhere to record a scan.** FIX-11's review list is read *after* the
   night by someone who was not holding the phone. `pendingCheckins` is per-device
   and drained on success; `tickets.checked_in_by` holds only the *first* operator
   (`20260228200000_ticket_checkin.sql:2-4`) and the second read writes nothing at
   all. The classification FIX-11 asks for needs facts the check-in path never
   persists: which device, which operator on the second read, and the interval.

2. **A refunded ticket does not exist.** All four refund writers delete the ticket
   row, and `ticket_refunds.ticket_id` is `NOT NULL REFERENCES public.tickets ON
   DELETE CASCADE` (`20260227200000_ticket_refunds.sql:3`). Owner decision: **Option
   B** — persist the refund evidence, keep deleting the ticket. Option A
   (soft-invalidate `tickets`, 63 call sites across 22 files) is recorded as
   deferred and is not to be drifted into partially.

3. **`attendances` has no party column.** `supabase/schema.sql:231-238` is
   `unique(event_id, user_id)`. A double bill is **one event with two parties**
   (owner decision), so a member checking in at both collides server-side today,
   every time. FIX-07 cannot be met without this.

4. **A guest-list entry cannot say who or when.** `guest_list_entries` carries
   `status` only, which is why `attendance/route.ts:129` hard-codes
   `checkedInAt: null`. The third outcome promises a fact; a blank where a fact is
   promised is worse than the weaker sentence.

Purpose: everything server-side in this phase depends on this migration. Nothing
after it can be verified without it, and `npm run build` will pass whether or not
it has been applied — the TypeScript types come from `src/types/database.ts`, not
from the live database. That false-positive is why Task 3 exists and blocks.

Output: one transaction-wrapped migration touching four tables, the matching
interfaces in the same commit, and the migration applied and observed.
</objective>
