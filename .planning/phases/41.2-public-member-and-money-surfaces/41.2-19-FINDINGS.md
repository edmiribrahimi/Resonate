## F-41.2-19-01 — **BLOCKING FOR THE PHASE GATE.** The declaration turned `verify:touch-targets` red on seven elements, and none of them is this plan's to repair

**This is the plan's one real divergence, and it is reported rather than papered
over.** Plan 41.2-19's own acceptance text says *"`npm run verify` reports 0
failed across the 15 gates that reach a verdict"*. **It does not.**

### What happened, mechanically

`verify-touch-targets` scopes itself to the **declared** surfaces. At this plan's
base commit the manifest held **35**, so the files reached only by
`/events/[slug]`, `/events/[slug]/menu` and `/dashboard` were **outside its
scope** and it exited 0. Declaring those three surfaces — which criterion 1
requires and check F now enforces — put **158 JSX files** in scope where there
had been fewer, and the gate measured **70 elements across 111 files** and found
**7 that declare no unprefixed minimum**.

```
$ node scripts/verify-touch-targets.mjs
  scope — 38 converted surface(s), 158 file(s) with JSX
  measured 70 element(s) across 111 file(s).
  FAILED — 7 element(s) do not declare the minimum:
$ echo $?
1
```

**This is the gate working, not a regression.** Wave 0 predicted **81** failing
elements under a 38-surface declaration (`41.2-WAVE0-FINDINGS.md` §5.1); waves 1
– 7 cleared 74 of them. The seven survivors were invisible while the surfaces
were undeclared, and they are visible now **because** the declaration landed.
Reverting the declaration to restore a green would be the debt-goes-quiet defect
in its purest form: the number would fall because the gate stopped looking.

### The seven, at `file:line`

| # | File:line | Element | What it actually is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `src/app/(public)/events/[slug]/RedeemConfirmationModal.tsx:207` | `<button>` | the **bartender's serve area** — `flex flex-1 flex-col …` inside a full-screen flex column. It is the **largest tap target in the product**; its height comes from `flex-1`, which the gate cannot read |
| 2 | `src/app/(public)/events/[slug]/RedeemConfirmationModal.tsx:244` | `<button>` | the **row that reverts a token** — `px-6 py-2 text-xs`, and it is small **on purpose**, so a bartender's tap cannot hit it by mistake |
| 3 | `src/app/(public)/events/[slug]/menu/GuestTokenDisplay.tsx:284` | `<button>` | the same serve area on the drinks menu, byte-identical in shape |
| 4 | `src/app/(public)/events/[slug]/menu/GuestTokenDisplay.tsx:322` | `<button>` | the same reverting row, same deliberate narrowness |
| 5 | `src/components/media/MediaUpload.tsx:578` | `<button>` | the **drop zone** — `flex w-full … p-8 border-2 border-dashed`. Its box is far above 44px and comes from padding |
| 6 | `src/components/media/MediaUpload.tsx:610` | `<input>` | a **hidden file input** — `type="file" className="hidden"`. It has no box at all. Exemption 7 covers `type="hidden"`; this is hidden by class |
| 7 | `src/components/media/MyMediaSection.tsx:179` | `<a>` | a link **wrapping a block** — `block mb-3`, whose height is its content's |

### Why this plan repaired none of them

**Four of the seven are inside the two files a person measured and declared
correct.** Elements 1–4 are exactly the screens D-41.2-06 and D-41.2-07 exempt.
The exemptions were written against `verify-dialogs`, about the **shell**;
nothing exempted the same screens' **controls** from `verify-touch-targets`, and
nobody wrote that down. **The exemptions cast a shadow on a second gate and the
shadow was not anticipated by any plan in this phase.** That is the finding, and
it is a decision for a person:

- **Raising the reverting row to 44px would defeat the argument that granted the
  exemption.** That row is narrow *so that a bartender's tap cannot hit it by
  mistake*; making it a 44px target is making it easier to hit, on the control
  that sends money backwards at a counter with a queue in front. **The gate and
  the domain want opposite things on that one element**, and this conflict is
  new — it did not exist while the surface was undeclared.
- **The serve area is the inverse case.** It is the biggest target in the
  product and the gate reds it for declaring no height. A gate that reddens the
  largest tap target in the product is reddening a correct file, which is §0
  rule 3's own definition of how a gate gets switched off.

**Three of the seven are ordinary unfinished work by waves that have already
run.** Elements 5–7 belong to plans 41.2-18 (`MediaUpload.tsx`, which cleared 2
of its 4) and 41.2-14 (`MyMediaSection.tsx`, which cleared 3 of its 4).

### What was NOT done, and why each road was refused

| Road | Refused because |
| --- | --- |
| edit the source files | **Out of scope by instruction.** This plan edits no file under `src/`; the waves that own those files have already run, and a reconciliation editing another wave's source is two authors on one line |
| widen a touch-target exemption | **T-41-42 tampering**, and the gate says so in its own output: *"Fix the ELEMENT, not this gate. Widening an exemption to clear a red is the tampering T-41-42 names"* |
| revert the three declarations | **The worst road available.** It restores a green by removing three converted surfaces from every gate's scope, leaves criterion 1 false, and makes check F's census impossible. A number that falls because the gate stopped looking is the defect this whole phase exists to remove |
| leave it unreported | not a road |

### The disposition this plan takes

**The declaration stays; the red stays; the seven are named at `file:line`
here.** Criterion 1 and criterion 3 close on their own terms — every surface
declared, every `REMAINING` list empty, every emptiness a written decision the
gate reads. **RESP-03's criterion 5 does not close**, and saying so is the point
of this finding.

**The owner's decision is needed on elements 1–4**, and it is a real one rather
than a formality: the domain argument that granted the exemption and the gate's
44px floor point in opposite directions on the reverting row. Elements 5–7 are a
straightforward repair somebody must schedule.

describing the thing beside it, **in the report a reader actually sees**.

because it was a latent instance of the class, not a typo introduced by this
