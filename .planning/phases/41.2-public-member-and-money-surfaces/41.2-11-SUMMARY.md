## 3. The heading — a written decision, and it is to stay headless

**The surface stays without a page heading, as a DECLARED EXCEPTION.** `41.2-RESEARCH.md`
assumption A6 says adopting the title primitive on the two headless pages is a **copy
decision, not a defect**, and that a deliberately headless reading needs a declared
exception. This is it, and it rests on three things:

1. **The omission is already a written decision in this file.** The comment the conversion
   preserved says the event title and date are *intentionally omitted* to keep the menu
   focused on drinks. A page heading naming the event reinstates exactly what that sentence
   removes — and reversing a product decision under cover of a layout change is the shape of
   deviation this phase keeps recording.
2. **There is no dominant line to promote.** §11's copy contract is *none introduced*. This
   surface's dominant object is a list of drinks; its sections carry their own headings
   already (`UserTokenDisplay.tsx:42` is one). A page title here would be copy invented by a
   conversion, which is the one thing §11 forbids. The only candidate string is the one
   `generateMetadata` composes, and putting it on screen is the same reversal as (1).
3. **The document is not nameless.** `generateMetadata` gives it the event's title and
   *Drink Menu*, which is the browser tab and the document announcement.

**The count, reported and never asserted as a threshold** — `PageTitle` renders the `<h1>`,
so a converted route file contains zero of them and a threshold on that count is
unsatisfiable:

```
$ LC_ALL=C /usr/bin/grep -c '<h1' <all eight files of this surface>
page.tsx:0   loading.tsx:0   PartyDrinkMenu.tsx:0   UserTokenDisplay.tsx:0
GuestTokenDisplay.tsx:0   GuestDrinkMenu.tsx:0   GuestLoginBanner.tsx:0   EventQRCode.tsx:0
```

**Checked across the whole SURFACE and not only the page file**, because a component in the
closure carrying the heading is exactly how adopting the primitive would have produced a
second one — the mistake §7.3 caught on `/newsletter`.

**Whether a guest standing at a bar should read a heading is the owner's question.** It is
recorded here as a question rather than answered by a diff, and it belongs on the human
sitting.

**Clear is on the quiet rung and not the destructive one, and that is 41.2-10's F-10-04
reasoning applied a second time:** the destructive rung is for acts that destroy, and this
act removes a value that one further action restores. Over-signalling a reversible step
spends the rung a genuinely irreversible one will need.

**The red hover hint was dropped, and its cost is disclosed rather than hidden.** Clearing
the explicit closing time falls the party back to its end time, and where there is none the
menu **never closes automatically** — which widens the window in which tokens can be bought
and, through the grace period, redeemed. The ladder has no hover-tint rung and inventing one
here would make this file a second author of the button contract, so the consequence is
written down (F-11-06) instead of being carried by a colour only a pointer can see. **What
the control sends did not change.**

**No hand-written switch track exists in this perimeter** — §2.5's measurement held; nothing
here needed the switch primitive, and none was adopted.
