## F-41.2-14-01 — a refusal that names one cause for two

**Where:** `src/components/auth/ResetPasswordButton.tsx:44` and `:66`, rendered
at `:86`.

**What.** `status` reaches `"error"` by two independent routes:

- `:44` — `if (!user?.email)`: the session resolved, but the account carries no
  address to send to.
- `:66` — `setStatus(error ? "error" : "sent")`: the provider refused the send.

Both draw the same four words at `:86`. A person who meets it cannot tell an
account without an address from a provider that would not send, and the two have
different next steps — the first is not retryable and the second is, while the
sentence says *try again* to both.

**Why it is recorded and not fixed.** Rewording a refusal changes what a person
reads, which is outside a visual conversion's remit. The wording was carried
across the conversion **byte-identical**, deliberately.

**Why it costs more than it looks.** This repository has **no error tracking**
(`meta-gates.md`, and `package.json` carries no monitoring dependency), so what
is on screen is the whole of what anybody will ever learn. It is the shape of
the newsletter defect already recorded in `.planning/codebase/CONCERNS.md` —
*"Qualcosa è andato storto"* over three different causes — one degree milder,
because here there are two causes rather than three.

**What closing it would take:** two sentences, one per cause, in the shape
`venues/EditVenueButton.tsx:76-85` uses — a total `Record` over the causes, so a
third cause becomes a compile error rather than a blank state. Needs an owner
decision on wording, because the sentences are what a person reads.

## F-41.2-14-02 — the incumbent delete control rendered an undecoded entity

**Where:** `src/components/media/MyMediaSection.tsx:170`, as the file stood
before this plan (commit `c5d60d2^`).

**What.** The line read, in an expression container:

```jsx
{pendingDelete === item.id ? "..." : "&#10005;"}
```

JSX decodes HTML entities in **text nodes**, not in **string literals**. Two
lines in the same file proved both halves of that: `:152` and `:192` wrote the
same kind of entity as JSX text and decoded correctly, while `:170` wrote it
inside a string and did not. So the delete control did not show a cross — it
showed the eight characters of the entity itself.

**Status: fixed as a side effect, not as a repair.** The control became the
shared icon rung, whose glyph is an inline `<svg>` path, so the string is gone
by construction rather than by being corrected. Recorded because the *class* of
defect survives the fix: an entity inside a string literal renders, compiles and
looks wrong only to somebody who opens the page. **A grep for entity sequences
inside string literals across the tree has not been run** and is not this plan's
scope.

## F-41.2-14-03 — the delete control could not be reached on a phone

**Where:** `src/components/media/MyMediaSection.tsx:167`, as it stood before
this plan.

**What.** The control was drawn `opacity-0` and revealed by `group-hover`. A
phone has no hover, and this is a **member surface read on a phone** — so the
only control that removes a member's own upload was, in practice, unreachable
there.

**Status: changed by this plan, and stated rather than buried.** A 44 px floor
declared on an invisible control would be a floor declared and not kept, so the
reveal went with the size: the control is now always visible, at the icon rung,
in the critical semantic over the tolerated translucent black scrim.

**What did not change:** the guard. `window.confirm` still stands between the
tap and `deleteMedia`, and the action itself was not opened. This is the
opposite direction from D-41.2-06's bartender revert — there, adopting the
primitive would have made a **money-reverting** control full-width under the
thumb with no confirmation, loosening a guard; here the guard is a modal
confirmation that is untouched, and what changed is that the control exists at
all on the device the surface is actually used on.
