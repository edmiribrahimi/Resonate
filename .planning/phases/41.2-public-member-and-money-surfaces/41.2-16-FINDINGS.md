## 2. Cross-domain impact, written before the diff

- **Venue secrecy.** This component is the **third branch** of the public event
  page's venue chain — the branch that renders the hint and never the address.
  Everything it renders is chosen so as **not** to be the address. A conversion
  may restyle it and may not narrow it. The rule the file states in place is that
  *the prose around the hint must not narrow it either — a hint made precise by
  its caption is a reveal with extra steps.* **A conversion adds no caption.**
- **Access and gating.** The three branches of the unlock chain correspond to
  three states of a reader — not signed in, signed in and not approved, approved.
  A merged branch is a reader shown a path that is not theirs, or shown one that
  is. `member` is not `approved`; the chain reads the second, and it keeps
  reading the second.
- **Community and membership.** The two bullets describe the two ways in. **They
  are not the same way**, and the file says so in place. Making them read the
  same would be a product statement about how somebody gets into a night, made
  by a visual conversion.
- **Next.js architecture.** The component is a client component today and stays
  one. Nothing is added to the client bundle that was not there: the two
  primitives it adopts are already in the bundle of every converted surface.

## 5. What may change, and the one direction that is safe

**The conversion is a strengthening in exactly one measurable respect**: four
controls with no declared minimum reach the 44px floor. **Nothing about who may
open this dialog changes; only how easily a finger hits it.**

**And the rung follows the act, not the file type.** In the previous phase's
reveal confirmation the confirming control moved to the *destructive* rung,
because revealing an address **is** the irreversible act and a heavier confirming
control makes it harder to press by accident. **Here there is no such control at
all**: this dialog's three answers are *a way in*, *a second way in*, and *a way
out*. None of them performs an irreversible act, and none of them is destructive.
Assigning the destructive rung to any of them would be a colour lying about a
consequence.
