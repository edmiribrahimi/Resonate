<objective>
Convert the guest money path at the bar: the panel a guest holds up to have a
drink token redeemed, and the menu they bought it from.

Purpose: **this is where the failure is not visual.** A guest who has paid holds
their proof in *their own browser's storage*, and three of this phase's four
recorded silent failures live on these two files: the write of that custody, the
read of it, and the network answer that says a token's status is unknown.
Everything they swallow ends in the same place — a person at a bar, in front of a
queue, holding a phone that says they bought nothing. `checkin-offline.md`'s
asymmetry applies exactly: refusing somebody who is entitled is worse than the
alternative, because the first failure happens in front of a line.

**None of the three is repaired here.** They are recorded at `file:line` in this
phase's `deferred-items.md` — by plan 41.2-01, in wave 0 — and routed to a plan
that owns what a buyer is told
when a purchase fails. A visual conversion that "improves" one has changed
behaviour on the money path under a visual mandate — stop condition 2 in a
different costume. There is **no error tracking** in this repository, so that
decision is a real cost and the plan states it rather than eliding it.

The panel is also the heaviest single client file in the bar, carrying a
hand-rolled dialog shell, **four** third-breakpoint uses of which one is not a
boundary decision at all, two hand-rolled placeholders, and a poll every few
seconds.

Output: two files, one dialog off the ratchet, four breakpoint uses resolved, and
a guest's custody proved untouched.
</objective>

## The custody assertions — after every diff that opens either file

A guest's proof of purchase lives in **their browser**, and every one of these
paths is a place a paid token becomes invisible. Assert each by text after the
diff, and quote it before and after:

1. **The storage key's construction is byte-identical.** A changed key is a wiped
   wallet for every guest who already has one, with no error anywhere.
2. **The write of the custody record is byte-identical**, including the catch that
   swallows a storage failure. Recorded, not repaired.
3. **The read of the custody record is byte-identical**, including the catch that
   returns an empty list — which is why *storage unavailable* and *you bought
   nothing* look the same to a guest.
4. **The polling interval and its catch are byte-identical**, including the value
   the network path returns when it cannot answer. A token that will never arrive
   and one that is about to look the same, and that is recorded rather than fixed.
</conversion_rules>
