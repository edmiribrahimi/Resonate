# Phase 41.2 Plan 10: Knot 1, the Shared Money Core — Summary

**The four files every drink redemption and every ticket purchase passes through were
converted once, in a wave of their own; seven third-breakpoint uses are gone because the
primitive owns that boundary; and no status transition, amount, idempotency key or webhook
path moved — asserted line by line rather than claimed.**

**Two of the four conversions the plan asked for were refused, and saying so is the
substance of this summary rather than a footnote.** The plan modelled
`RedeemConfirmationModal.tsx` as a twin of `RefundActions.tsx` with one shell at the foot of
the file. Measured, it holds **three** overlays, and two of them are the screens a bartender
operates. Converting those would have moved the control that sends money backwards from a
deliberately narrow pill to a full-width control under the thumb. That is a behaviour change
on the money path wearing a visual costume, so it did not happen.

**Why no marker — and this is a refused acceptance criterion, stated as one.** The plan asks
for exactly one marker, *on the Cancel control*. The confirmation has no Cancel: its two
controls are a close `×` and `Confirm`. Declaring a marker would have meant **adding a Cancel
control**, which is a new user-visible word on a money confirmation — precisely what
T-41.2-58 is in the register to prevent. `meta-gates.md` resolves contradicting gates in
favour of the more restrictive, and a rule that forbids an act is more restrictive than one
that requires one.

The intent is met all the same, and it is measurable: the primitive's documented fallback
(`Dialog.tsx:117-147`) focuses the close control, which is first in the DOM and the least
destructive control by construction. **Enter therefore closes; it never confirms.** The
perimeter already carries this precedent with its reason written down at
`GuestLoginBanner.tsx:53-60`.

**And the affirmative control kept the accent fill rather than taking the destructive rung.**
`activate` moves a token `purchased → active` and the screen it opens carries an explicit path
back to `purchased` — the step is reversible by design. The destructive rung is for acts that
destroy; this one claims a drink somebody already bought. Full reasoning in
`41.2-10-FINDINGS.md` F-10-04.


The plan's `<read_first>` says *"Its shell is at `:167-168`"*. There are three, and only the
third is the one the plan describes.

The other two are refused, and the file argues its own case — both sentences survive the diff
unchanged:

```
:126  {/* Big tap-to-serve area: takes the whole screen above the Cancel row */}
:150  {/* Cancel row, kept narrow so the bartender's tap can't hit it by mistake */}
```

Inside the primitive the serve area becomes a panel body and the **revert** control becomes a
full-width control in the actions region — directly under the thumb. Reverting an active token
sends money backwards at a bar with a queue in front of it, so a conversion that makes it
*easier* to hit moves the guard the wrong way. The plan's own impact analysis is the standard
it fails: *"the conversion makes the guards tighter rather than looser."*

**Their inks were converted; their geometry was not.** A token substitution moves nothing on
screen but the colour, so both screens now carry semantic ink — and both files reach **0** raw
palette and **0** legacy tokens.

**Consequences, stated rather than hidden:**

- The dialog ratchet's entry for it is correctly **not** STALE, and `REMAINING` fell 5 → 4

- **The overlays were left legible to the matcher rather than rewritten into a shape it
  cannot see.** Making the number go quiet without the thing changing is the fifth mechanism
  this phase recorded in wave 4, and it is not available as a shortcut here.
