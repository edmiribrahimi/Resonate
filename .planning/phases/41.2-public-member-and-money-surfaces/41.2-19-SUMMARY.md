## 3. How the two exemptions were written

**In the gate, not in a summary, and with two arguments rather than one argument
cited twice.** *An exemption without its reason is indistinguishable from a file
nobody got to* — which is exactly the distinction this gate already draws between
**exempt** (a person measured it and declared it correct) and **fenced** (nobody
measured it at all).

Each file entered **two** constants, and both were required:

- **`DECLARED_EXCEPTIONS`** — the list the gate prints, in full, on every run,
  above every count.
- **`NEVER_MEASURED_BY_B`** — the Map check B's loop actually reads. **A file in
  the printed list but absent from the Map would be opened, found carrying a
  shell, and reported as an UNDECLARED COPY** — a red on a file a person measured
  and declared correct. WR-01 in that gate's own header is the record of the two
  lists drifting the other way.

### Entry 3 — `RedeemConfirmationModal.tsx` (D-41.2-06, the owner's)

> exempt from check B for TWO of its three overlays […] The reason is the file's
> own, written before this phase: the serve area *"takes the whole screen"* so a
> bartender *"doesn't have to aim"*, and the row that reverts the token is *"kept
> narrow so the bartender's tap can't hit it by mistake"*. Inside the primitive
> that reverting row becomes a full-width control in the actions region, directly
> under the thumb — money going backwards at a counter, at two in the morning,
> with a queue in front and the guard LOOSENED instead of tightened. […] **NOT
> extended to any other file by analogy**

### Entry 4 — `GuestTokenDisplay.tsx` (D-41.2-07, under that rule, on its own argument)

> exempt from check B for TWO of its three shells — D-41.2-07, granted under
> D-41.2-06's **RULE** and on **THIS** file's own argument, which plan 41.2-12
> re-derived after **explicitly declining to inherit** the twin's decision. **The
> argument differs from the twin's and runs in BOTH directions**: the serve area
> *"takes the whole screen above the Cancel row"*, so inside the primitive it
> becomes a panel body at the panel's own width — **the bartender HAS TO AIM** —
> while the narrow Cancel becomes a full-width control under the thumb. The guard
> would loosen on the reversible act and tighten on the irreversible one […]
> Both shells were kept **LEGIBLE** to the matcher throughout, so `REMAINING`
> never fell for a reason nobody wrote down.

**The two arguments are genuinely different.** The twin's is one-directional —
the reverting control gets bigger. This one is two-directional — the serve area
gets *smaller* and the reverting control gets *bigger* at the same time. A
refusal that declines an available precedent and re-derives the argument is worth
more than one that cites it.

**A latent defect found while doing this:** the gate printed
`declared exceptions: 2` as a **string literal** above a list it never measured.
A literal `2` above a four-entry list is the smallest possible version of the
defect this gate is written against, *in the report a reader actually sees*. It
is `DECLARED_EXCEPTIONS.length` now (F-41.2-19-05).
